Pour-over is how most specialty coffee bars show off a single-origin coffee: hot water, a paper filter, and control over every step. At home it's cheap to start and hard to master, and the gear decides which part is hard.

You need three things: a dripper, a gooseneck kettle (for a slow, aimed pour), and a scale. Skip the scale and you're guessing. Skip the gooseneck and you're flooding the bed.

Tip from behind the bar

Start at a 1:16 ratio (for example 20g coffee to 320g water), water just off the boil, about 3 minutes total. Too sour? Grind finer. Too bitter? Grind coarser.

Questions people ask

V60 or Chemex?

V60 for one or two cups and more control; Chemex for brewing a pot to share and a cleaner, lighter cup.

Do I need a gooseneck kettle?

For pour-over, yes. A regular kettle dumps water too fast to control extraction.

What grinder pairs with this?

Any burr grinder from our grinder guide. The Baratza Encore is the easy default.
