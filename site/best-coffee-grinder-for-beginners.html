The Best Coffee Grinder for Beginners

If you only upgrade one thing, upgrade this. Here's which grinder to buy for how you actually brew.

Ask anybody who works a coffee bar what to buy first and you'll hear the same answer: a burr grinder. Not a fancier machine. Not pricier beans. A grinder. Pre-ground coffee goes stale fast, and blade grinders chop beans into dust and boulders that brew unevenly, which is where a lot of 'bitter and sour at the same time' coffee comes from.

A burr grinder crushes beans to a consistent size. That one change makes cheap beans taste better and good beans taste like the bag said they would.

Questions people ask

Do I really need a burr grinder?

If you're happy with your coffee, no. If it's ever bitter, sour, or flat, a burr grinder is the fix that does the most per dollar.

Can one grinder do espresso and drip?

Some can (the Fellow Opus is built for it), but espresso needs very fine, very small adjustments. Most beginner grinders are tuned for filter coffee.

Hand grinder or electric?

Hand grinders are quiet and often grind more evenly per dollar, but cranking for a full 10-cup pot gets old. Electric if you brew for a family.
