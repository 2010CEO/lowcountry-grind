Most coffee people drink at home comes out of a drip machine, and there's no shame in that. The difference between a good machine and a bad one is mostly about water temperature and how evenly it wets the grounds.

Cheap machines often brew too cool, which makes thin, sour coffee. The picks below either brew properly hot or are built around getting it right.

Tip from behind the bar

Use about 2 tablespoons (10g) of coffee per 6 oz cup, and clean the machine with a descaler every month or two. If your water is hard, scale builds up faster than you'd think, so descale more often.

Questions people ask

Thermal or glass carafe?

Thermal keeps coffee hot without cooking it. Glass on a hot plate is fine if you drink it within 30 minutes.

What is SCA certification?

The Specialty Coffee Association tests home brewers for brew temperature, time, and evenness. It's a good shortcut to 'this brews properly.'

Does the grinder matter for drip?

Yes. A burr grinder improves any drip machine more than a pricier machine would.
